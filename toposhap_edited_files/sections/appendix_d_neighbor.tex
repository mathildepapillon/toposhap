\subsection{Architecture Selection, Baselines, and Compute}
\label{app:selection}

\input{tables/performance_summary}

\textbf{Sweeps.} The GCCN sweep is a TopoTune-style neighborhood grid
of 22--25 configurations per dataset: the saved record's per-dataset
draws on the eight datasets it covered, and one fixed 23-configuration
draw reused verbatim on the three datasets swept for this paper
(per-dataset counts in \cref{fig:lead}'s legend; the configuration
lists ship with the released pipeline). The HOPSE sweep is its
published 12-configuration grid. Sweep winners are selected per seed
by validation accuracy and charged the full grid.

\input{tables/shortlist_baselines}

\textbf{Shortlist controls.} Each control starts from the same
five-epoch initial model and continues training under the standard
settings of the selection pipeline. The random $B{=}2$ control draws
two architectures uniformly at random from the 2047 non-empty
neighborhood subsets (draw seeded by dataset and seed, redrawing
duplicates), continues both, and selects between them by validation
accuracy: two continuations, like the selection pipeline, but without
backward elimination's 66 validation passes. The full-model default
continues one model, the all-neighborhood architecture, with no search
and no selection step.

\textbf{Compute accounting.}
\label{app:flops-conventions}
All FLOPs are counted under one convention: PyTorch's
\texttt{FlopCounterMode} with dense pricing, explicit formulas for
operations the counter does not price (eigendecomposition, SVD, sparse
products), and every stage charged, including HOPSE's encoding
preprocessing. Runs that predate the profiler are priced by rebuilding
the recorded architecture, profiling one training epoch and the
evaluation passes under the same convention, and combining these
per-epoch counts with the recorded epoch and evaluation tallies; we
describe such costs as profiler-based reconstructions. Exact
neighborhood attribution requires all $2^{11} = 2048$ masked
validation passes; backward elimination requires $(n^{2}+n)/2 = 66$;
sampled cell attribution requires at most $Tn + 1$ forward passes per
instance, with $T$ counting every evaluated ordering
(\cref{app:cell-intervention,tab:runtime}). Wall-clock comparisons run
on one NVIDIA H200 GPU per run.

\subsection{Sensitivity Analyses}
\label{app:sensitivity}

\textbf{Initial-training length.} \Cref{tab:stem-sensitivity} repeats
the $B{=}2$ selection with the initial model trained for 3, 5
(default), or 10 epochs. Mean selected test accuracy moves by at most
0.012 on Benzene and NCI1 and by 0.036 on MUTAG; the selected
neighborhood sets themselves differ across settings.

\input{tables/stem_sensitivity}

\textbf{Selection metric on MANTRA.} A separately run three-seed
variant replaces ordinary validation accuracy with balanced validation
accuracy in the masked game, the threshold's $p$, and the final
comparison ($\tau$'s binomial form is then an approximation, since
balanced accuracy is not a binomial proportion). It selects 11, 11,
and 9 neighborhoods across seeds 42--44 and reaches $0.804 \pm 0.011$
balanced test accuracy, against $0.615 \pm 0.103$ for the original
plain-accuracy run on the same seeds, consistent with ordinary
validation accuracy rewarding majority-class predictions on this
imbalanced dataset. \Cref{fig:lead,tab:performance-summary} report the
original plain-accuracy selection.

\subsection{Exhaustive Benchmark and Attribution Agreement}
\label{app:benchmark}

The controlled evaluation of \cref{sec:performance-controlled} uses
all $2^{6} = 64$ subsets of the first six neighborhoods of \cref{app:selection-pipeline}, each trained separately from scratch to the standard budget, under twelve dataset/hyperparameter settings and
seeds 42--44; the empty subset is a trained model with node features only. The twelve settings are the four GraphXAI molecule datasets, MUTAG, NCI1, NCI109, the two ZINC configurations of
\cref{app:training}, and the three citation graphs. Seven settings were available while the selection procedure was developed; five (MUTAG, NCI109, Alkane-Carbonyl, and the two ZINC configurations) were held out and first evaluated after the procedure was fixed.

\label{app:attr-agreement}
\Cref{tab:attr-agreement} compares exact Shapley attributions over the
six neighborhoods computed two ways: from masked evaluations of the
five-epoch model (the logit column scores validation logits, the
primary comparison; the accuracy column re-scores the masked side by
validation accuracy) and from the separately trained subsets' test
values. Agreement is the Spearman correlation between seed-averaged
attribution profiles; stability is the mean $\pm$ sd of the per-seed
correlations, which mixes seed-to-seed variation of the masked profile
into the same statistic and is reported separately for that reason.
The pooled row correlates $12 \times 6 = 72$ points (one per setting
and neighborhood, each a three-seed mean); intervals are 95\%
bootstrap over the twelve setting blocks (10{,}000 resamples), and the
primary statistic's permutation $p$ is $0.048$ (20{,}000
permutations). The two games differ in split and score (validation
logits or accuracy versus test accuracy or negative MAE), a mismatch
the saved record does not allow us to remove.

\subsection{Oracle Evaluation of Candidate Generation}
\label{app:selection-evidence}

\Cref{tab:oracle-generation} reruns backward elimination with each
removal scored by the benchmark's seed-averaged test values, per
setting. Because it consults test scores, this analysis is an oracle
diagnostic of the candidate generator; it is not a validation-only
application of the practical method, and it does not validate the
masked scores the practical pipeline uses. On the five held-out
settings the greedy path contains the benchmark's best architecture in
four, with worst regret $+0.0068$ (the GIN-backbone ZINC recipe); on
the seven development settings it matches the best architecture's
test value in five (on NCI1 with a size-5 subset rather than the
size-3 optimum) and trails by at most $+0.0042$.
